# The API-version floor rises to meet the ceiling, and every behaviour guard moves to `honours`

The Extractor API-version pin was `≥ 2 and ≤ 7`. The ceiling had been raised deliberately, on evidence, and recorded ([ADR-0021](./0021-verified-against-extractor-api-version-7.md)). The floor had not moved since it was set, and by the time it was looked at again it was authorising back-compat paths for hosts that no installation runs: an Extractor with no `authenticated_as` on `GET /status`, one whose poll reports no `chunks_done`, one that silently ignores `strict`. Production runs `Status_Controller::API_VERSION = 7` (Kntnt Extractor 0.7.0). Everything between 2 and 6 was a compatibility claim carried on inspection alone — the exact failure mode ADR-0021 exists to name, a number that reads as verification without a verification behind it.

**The floor is raised to 7.** Floor and ceiling are now the same number, and this ADR supersedes ADR-0021's consequence that "`FLOOR` is unchanged at `2`".

## Why one accepted version is coherent rather than brittle

`api_version` bounds the sealed container's byte layout, segment framing, segments-per-resource, sealed index, and reassembly order, and nothing else ([Extractor ADR-0017](https://github.com/Kntnt/kntnt-extractor/blob/main/docs/adr/0017-api-version-bounds-the-artifact-contract.md)). It therefore moves exactly when this client must stop and be re-verified. Accepting one version is not a narrow pin around an arbitrary release; it is the honest statement of what is known — *verified against the artifact contract as of `api_version` 7, and nothing else* — and the stop on anything else is the mechanism working, not failing.

The justification for paying that price now is that there is no installed base to protect. There is one user of this client and the use so far has been testing, so a floor guarding compatibility guards zero installations. A claim that costs nothing to keep honest today costs a deletion round for every release it survives.

## The finding that reshaped the change: a floor guarantees no behaviour

"The floor rose, therefore this back-compat path is dead" is valid only where the version number genuinely is the discriminator. The Extractor ships caller-visible behaviour **additively, without moving `api_version`** — that is Extractor ADR-0017's whole point, and the `honours` list on the authenticated `GET /status` exists because a version number cannot answer the question. A build reporting `api_version: 7` may lack any honours-advertised behaviour.

So the change splits in two, and only the first half is a deletion.

- **The identity report is version-discriminated.** `authenticated_as` and `capabilities` landed at API version 4 and are not `honours` names; a build at 7 necessarily carries them. The degraded-behaviour bullet, the "fallback for an older Extractor" wrong-password step that stood in for the identity report, and the `GET /audit-log` comparison that used to disambiguate a `403` are all unreachable at the floor, and are gone. The wrong-password test itself survives, in `docs/implementation-notes.md`'s troubleshooting table, where it answers the question it is actually good for: *why* a request authenticated as nobody.
- **`chunks_done`, `strict`, `chunk_size`, `packaging_skips`, and `disclosure` are behaviour-discriminated.** All five are names on production's `honours` list. Their guards and their degraded-behaviour bullets are re-anchored to that list, or to the member's own presence in a response, and none of them is dead. The `strict` bullet is the clearest case: it used to fire *unconditionally*, on the stated grounds that no version number distinguishes a host that honours the member from one that ignores it. That sentence was true of the version number and false of `honours` — which was added for precisely this — so the bullet now fires on the list, and that half of the change did not depend on the floor raise at all.

The degraded-behaviour section is therefore four bullets, every one of them keyed on `honours`, and the run report's own lead-in says so.

### The trap this nearly walked into

The specification credited the API-version floor for the define-disclosure by-value fallback: the by-value rule "survives only as the fallback for an Extractor predating the discriminator, which this client still supports because its API-version floor is 2", and [ADR-0023](./0023-a-defines-disclosure-is-read-from-the-protocol-member.md) said in as many words that raising the floor above 6 would make the fallback, the `value_withheld_pre_protocol` reason, and its report branch dead code to delete together.

That attribution is wrong, and following it would have deleted a live defence. The mechanism is **presence-based**: `discovery.py` omits the `disclosure` key entirely when the server sent none, and its own docstring says why — *"The omission is the payload: it is how the classifier tells a server that implements the disclosure protocol from one that predates it."* `disclosure` is an `honours` name, so a build at `api_version: 7` may still not send it. The fallback stays reachable at any floor. Both surfaces are corrected to name member presence, and neither behaviour changed.

## The verification, not the edit

ADR-0021 said it of the ceiling and it holds here: raising a number without checking what it now asserts launders an unverified contract through a test that reads as verification. A floor at 7 asserts that every endpoint and member this client depends on exists at `api_version` 7. That was checked against the Extractor's own source at `c513388` (`v0.7.0`, `Status_Controller::API_VERSION = 7`), not inferred from a green suite — the suite reads this repository's surfaces, never the Extractor.

- **Every endpoint on the pinned surface is registered.** `GET /status` (`Status_Controller`), `GET /environment` (`Environment_Controller`), `GET /tables` (`Tables_Controller`), `GET /files` (`Files_Controller`), and `POST`/`GET /extractions`, `GET`/`DELETE /extractions/{id}`, `POST /extractions/{id}/consume` (`Extractions_Controller`), plus the `manage_options`-only `GET /audit-log` (`Audit_Log_Controller`).
- **Every request member this client sends exists.** `tables`, `tables_structure_only`, `files`, `public_key`, `strict`, and `chunk_size` are all read by `Extractions_Controller::create()`; `state=all` is a registered `args` entry on the listing.
- **Every response member this client reads exists.** `api_version`, `authenticated_as`, `capabilities`, and `honours` on `GET /status`; `php_version`, `server_software`, `core_version`, `home_url`, `site_url`, `table_prefix`, `content_dir`, `uploads_dir`, the `database` scalars, `active_plugins`, `dropins`, and the per-define `value`/`disclosure` pair on `GET /environment`; `name`/`rows`/`bytes` on `GET /tables`; `{ path, size, mtime }` plus the paging `cursor` on `GET /files`; `id`, `state`, `download_url`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, `skipped_files`, and `progress`'s five counters — `tables_done`, `tables_total`, `files_done`, `files_total`, `chunks_done` — on the job endpoints.
- **Every error code this client branches on exists.** `kntnt_extractor_not_authenticated`, `kntnt_extractor_missing_operate_capability`, `kntnt_extractor_missing_manage_capability`, `kntnt_extractor_restricted_path`, `kntnt_extractor_unknown_resource`, `kntnt_extractor_selection_too_large`, `kntnt_extractor_payload_too_large`, and the locked-job refusals the consume and cancel retries read.
- **`HONOURED_BEHAVIOURS` at 0.7.0 is** `attempts`, `chunk_size`, `chunks_done`, `disclosure`, `packaging_skips`, `selection_limits`, `skipped_files`, `state`, `strict`, `timings`, `unknown_resource_names` — which is what makes the split above a reading of the source rather than a guess about it.

**What this verification is not.** No `api_version: 7` container was unsealed here, exactly as at the ceiling raise; the artifact contract itself rests on ADR-0021's own source inspection, which this ADR does not re-open and does not strengthen. What is new is the *floor's* claim, and that claim is about surface existence, which source inspection answers completely.

## Consequences

- `FLOOR` and `VERIFIED_CEILING` are both `7` in `tests/test_api_version_ceiling_consistency.py`. Every pinning surface states both bounds; a new test refuses a stale floor left standing on any live surface, on `CONTEXT.md`, or on `README.md`. The ADRs and `CHANGELOG.md` are outside that scan on purpose — they are dated records of what was true when written, and rewriting them to match a raise is the opposite of the point.
- **No behaviour is reduced.** The widened stall window still fires when `chunks_done` is absent from the poll; the define-disclosure by-value fallback still fires when the record carries no discriminator; `strict` and `chunk_size` are still sent unconditionally, never gated on `honours`. Only guards expressed as version comparisons, and prose expressed as version claims, moved.
- **`scripts/unseal.py` is untouched**, including its tolerance of an Extractor emitting exactly one segment per table. The floor now refuses such an install outright, so that tolerance is defence in depth rather than a supported configuration, and `docs/implementation-notes.md` says so — deleting a reader that has not been re-tested would be a speculative change, which ADR-0021 already ruled against.
- **The next Extractor release stops the run.** That is the designed behaviour: `api_version` 8 will mean the container's shape moved, and the correct response is to examine it, raise both numbers together, and record what was checked — not to widen the floor back out to buy room.
